# RATAN

## Identity, naming, and evidence boundaries

RATAN, also styled **Ratan**, is described across the source material as a cash-settlement platform, service family, settlement-processing environment, settlement application, settlement-processing system, downstream settlement and cashflow-processing system, payment-processing system, application, technology-service name, central product, business application, and—within a certificate-authority migration tracker—a CI.

The **RATAN - About App** application profile describes RATAN as an in-house strategic cross-asset post-trade processing platform for FMO. Under [[strategic-fm-re-platforming-sfmrp]], it is intended to support migration of the Derivatives business from [[murex-g2000]] to FMRP strategic stacks.

The **RATAN - App Docs** documentation register associates RATAN with user guidance for Settlement, Korea, and Trade, as well as formal service-governance and operational-planning artifacts:

- An `ASRM` record.
- Settlement, Korea, and Trade user guides.
- An SLA and an OLA.
- A disaster-recovery plan and service-restore plan.
- A capacity-management plan.
- A test-environment specification.

The recovery, restore, and capacity records identify the service as `RATAN (51358)`. These references establish documentation associations only; they do not establish current approval status, operational effectiveness, or detailed recovery, capacity, or test-environment processes.

The [[5-ratan--11-ratan-ola--11-ratan-ola--13lq67q]] OLA source identifies `RATAN` as the central application or platform named in that document, but does not establish whether it is identical to [[ratanone]], a predecessor, or a separate system.

The **RATAN Confluence navigation index** identifies RATAN as the named application or documentation scope and links application overview, architecture, capacity, core functions, infrastructure, interfaces, operations, monitoring, projects, releases, security, and service restarts. It does not describe RATAN's implementation, business function, ownership, or lifecycle.

The **RATAN Knowledge Base** is a separate discovery and knowledge-management index. It uses RATAN as its central system name and links knowledge-transfer materials, including **RATAN - KT Sessions**, but provides no technical description or evidence of specific functionality, interfaces, account processing, ownership, or operational controls. The KT Sessions page must be ingested separately before its contents are treated as evidence.

The Knowledge Base links [[ratan-interface-inventory]] and [[ratan-interface-architecture]], but is a discovery source rather than an authoritative replacement for their underlying specifications. Its linked **What is VOSTRO/NOSTRO?** page may be relevant to banking terminology, but does not establish that RATAN processes VOSTRO or NOSTRO accounts or has a RATAN-specific account model or workflow.

The wiki contains [[ratanone]] and multiple `ratan-*` services. The navigation index, application profile, and Knowledge Base do not establish whether RATAN and RatanOne are identical, related, or distinct. The documentation register's `RATAN ONE` OLA title and `Ratan One` Korea processing-guide title suggest a close relationship, but are insufficient to prove a single canonical service identity. ## Settlement controls, accounting, and statuses

### Auto DVP and release-time operations

For [[auto-dvp]], the **Auto DVP (eBBS)** requirement states that RATAN consumes qualifying EBBS receipt notifications, validates a receive cashflow, links it to an eligible pay cashflow, and closes the pay cashflow's DVP exception. Non-qualifying, ambiguous, failed, withdrawn, and out-of-scope cases remain for manual Operations handling. This is distinct from [[ebbs-payment-accounting-integration]], which concerns accounting messages and posting.

The **OPS Allowed Actions Post Pending Release** source states that only `READY` payments may be sent downstream and release stops following transition to another status. On 13 January 2026, ad-hoc netting overlapped automatic release of the same gross cashflow; both gross and netted payments were released. The source characterizes this as an undocumented edge case and missing required system function. It does not establish serialization or atomicity. See [[cashflow-release-and-netting-race-condition]] and [[release-time-cashflow-status-gating]].

### Aspire accounting

For the interface described in **Cash Settlement - Aspire Accounting**, RATAN is specified to generate [[aspire]] payment-accounting entries and EOD CSV files delivered through [[fileit]], evaluate accounting eligibility, create new or reversal entries, maintain accounting status, apply entity static data, and generate business-day files under [[aspire-eod-accounting-file-cutoff]]. These are interface-specific requirements, not verified production behavior or general settlement behavior.

### Filtering and replication

The **SUSPENDED vs PROJECTED cashflow status in Ratan** requirement assigns complex or dynamic logic to Ratan while simple static volume-reduction rules remain in [[stella]]. For FXO-generated FXD, Ratan must retain `PROJECTED` cashflow where required for netting with `LNBR` and `CCS`. It also states Ratan supports front-end rule editing and maker-checker manual STP of suspended cashflows. It does not define authoritative lifecycle, precedence, or exact meanings of “filtered” and “aborted.”

The `READY` release rule is separate. Neither source establishes a unified status model, ordering, or atomicity mechanism. FXU Option 3's unspecified status hard block likewise does not establish a relationship to `READY`.

### Nostro and SSI stamping

The **Nostro Centralization** requirement identifies impacted Ratan functions: cashflow or trade stamping with a Nostro query, and accounting Nostro query. Ratan is expected to integrate with [[ssi-plus|SSI+]] after centralized Nostro static-data maintenance. It says Ratan uses `NOS` whereas [[razor|Razor]] uses `Nostro`, without establishing whether this is display, enumeration, or a broader incompatibility. It does not identify ownership for stamping, accounting lookup, notification consumption, local refresh/cache, or historical migration.

The **RFI Nostro stamping based on Portfolio** requirement proposes RATAN select RFI Nostro by booking entity, currency, and portfolio; preserve RFI stamping during Vostro stamping; create exceptions for ambiguous or mismatched instructions; treat RFI/non-RFI portfolio transitions as economic amendments; expose Nostro Type in ad-hoc SSI selection and cashflow details; and propagate RFI accounts to SWIFT output and RFI/non-RFI EBBS accounting. It affects cashflow stamping only; trade stamping remains BAU pending a separate strategic solution. These are not implementation or deployment evidence. See [[portfolio-based-rfi-nostro-stamping]], [[rfi-portfolio-economic-amendment]], [[nostro-type-static-data-model]], [[rfi-swift-account-propagation]], [[cashflow-versioning]], and [[ssi-stamping-behavior-differences]].

The trade-stamping BAU statement and Nostro Centralization's identification of cashflow-or-trade stamping address different scopes and do not establish a common future-state model.

## Netting and cashflow IDs

### Netting

The netting sources describe requirements and workflows, not confirmed implementation or deployment unless explicitly stated.

The **Netting Service - GUI & API integration** requirement describes RATAN as responsible for scoped GUI and backend workflows. For manual netting/un-netting, RATAN accepts GUI requests, re-queries current cashflow data before validation and execution, creates resultants, updates component and resultant states, and generates or closes related exceptions. It consumes `Withdrawal` and `New` events from [[stella]] for automatic un-netting when a netted component changes economically. Whether a resultant has been released or settled through [[razor]] changes the reversal path. See [[netting-api-contract]] and [[settle-as-gross-maker-checker-workflow]].

The **Netting Story Board** requires Ratan support FMO maker/checker profiles and limits, manual maker/checker release of net cashflows, netting information from [[gtss]], Net-to-Gross routing to [[nstp]], Net-ID many-to-one [[tlm]] matching for Bridge suspense reconciliation, CLS netting, and inter-entity netting through [[lcm]].

Scope stated as shared with [[s2bng]] includes ad-hoc netting, eligibility validation, Net-to-Gross [[tcrm]] workflow, pre-release controls, component review following amendments, display, audit, client configuration, and batch-netting requirements. Its post-release netting block is a soft block with incremental posting pending; deployed enforcement is unconfirmed. The 13 January 2026 double-release scenario is separate and does not establish Story Board implementation.

### Cashflow ID management

The cashflow-ID requirement describes Ratan as a cash-settlement platform or service family that consumes IDs from upstream TP systems, normalizes certain external IDs to 12 characters, and generates IDs for resultant netting and split cashflows. Generated IDs must be unique across services and processes: netting results use `N` and split results `S`, each followed by an auto-incrementing 11-digit value. The source does not prescribe atomicity, durability, or recovery; see [[how-is-ratan-cashflow-id-uniqueness-enforced]].

## Manual-entity settlement, messaging, and hard blocks

The manual-entity onboarding checklist describes RATAN as the settlement-processing environment for entities routed to RATAN rather than [[razor]]. For such entities, RATAN generates SWIFT messages and settlement accounting. Onboarding includes entity routing, SWIFT setup, release-time configuration, accounting, static data, business rules, network access, and test sign-off. It does not establish that every listed control is implemented within RATAN. See [[manual-entity-settlement-onboarding]] and [[entity-routing-and-cashflow-suppression]].

The Bahrain SCB UAT identifies RATAN as source of inbound settlement messages. [[fmsgw]] receives messages and forwards supported ones to [[amh]]. Tested families were `MT103`, `MT202`, `MT202COV`, and `MT192/292`; FMSGW returns ACK messages to RATAN for normal and approval-gated paths. It does not supply payloads, statuses, correlation keys, or timestamps.

The Qatar SCB Doha UAT identifies RATAN as upstream settlement and cashflow platform originating settlement messages submitted to FMSGW for AMH delivery. Nominal delivery and ACK-return paths passed for `MT103`, `MT202`, `MT202COV`, `MT192`, and `MT292`. It does not define retry, correlation, idempotency, or unsuccessful-ACK handling. See [[ratan-fmsgw-amh-settlement-message-routing]] and [[ratan-cashflow-lifecycle-service]].

Bahrain and Qatar are separate UAT scopes: Bahrain characterizes RATAN as inbound-message source and includes approval-gated ACK paths; Qatar characterizes it as upstream originator. They do not establish a single generalized routing contract.

The `SWAP_AGENT` hard-blocker UAT identifies RATAN as the referenced settlement application. For in-scope Coupon and Interim MTM cashflows or resultants with `Hard block Swap Agent` exception, RATAN prevents maker submission and checker approval for release. Permitted actions are Swift Suppressed, Failed, Hold, Cashflow Suppressed, and Unnet for resultants. Hard-blocker NSTP rules are made live via Settlement NSTP Rules Blotter maker/checker workflow. See [[swap-agent-hard-blocker]] and [[swap-agent-payment-type-netting-control]].

## Source limitations and unresolved ownership

Sources variously describe RATAN as a platform, application, service family, environment, product, business application, and CI. These descriptions remain source-specific and do not form a single technical or ownership model.

- Knowledge Base, navigation, and SME indexes are discovery/documentation sources, not technical specifications.
- The certificate migration tracker establishes only the listed EJBCA-to-MSPKI migration status, not wider certificate, vulnerability, or PID-remediation completion.
- The application profile gives business and operational profile information, not complete architecture.
- Requirements and delivery plans describe intended or scoped behavior, not necessarily production deployment.
- UAT evidence supports stated tests, not complete interface contracts.
- FXU options assign differing responsibilities and cannot be generalized.
- Korea sources use different interface directions and levels of detail.
- Bahrain and Qatar cover separate routing scopes.
- Nostro Centralization and portfolio-based RFI stamping have different scopes.
- Historical Stella mapping and netting `Withdrawal/New` handling are separate.
- `READY` gating, unspecified status hard blocks, and netting controls are not established as one unified lifecycle or atomicity model.
- The explicitly `OLD` restart guide requires current-state validation and does not establish current inventory, ownership, dependencies, or governance.
