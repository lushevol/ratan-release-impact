# Manual-Entity Settlement Operational Readiness

Manual-entity settlement operational readiness is the state in which a settlement capability can be safely deployed and supported in production. It extends beyond functional testing to cover the operational controls, accountable teams, support procedures, data and configuration readiness, and management of residual risks required for go-live.

## Distinguishing evidence types

- **UAT coverage** establishes whether agreed scenarios were tested and what results were recorded. It is tracked separately in [[tranche-1-uat-coverage-status]].
- **Migration readiness** concerns whether deployment prerequisites, data, configurations, dependencies, and cutover preparations are complete.
- **Operational readiness** concerns whether production processing can be monitored, supported, controlled, reconciled, and remediated.
- **Post-go-live issue management** records defects or operational gaps identified after release, assigns ownership, and supplies closure evidence.

No one category alone proves the others. For example, UAT execution is not by itself evidence that operational support controls are ready or that post-go-live risks have been closed.

Because the tracker body is unavailable, it does not establish any country-specific readiness or deployment outcome.

## Minimum tracker evidence

A usable readiness and issue tracker would normally identify:

- the affected country, entity, service, and settlement flow;
- the readiness domain or issue category;
- severity, impact, and residual risk;
- accountable owner and target date;
- mitigation, remediation, and dependency details;
- decision or acceptance authority;
- current state and closure criteria;
- evidence of validation after remediation.

The supplied source does not confirm that these fields or controls exist. Their retrieval remains an open investigation in [[what-were-the-tranche-1-operational-readiness-and-post-go-live-issues]].
