**VOSTRO** is a banking term derived from Latin meaning "Your Account." It is commonly used in international banking to refer to an account opened by another financial institution at the bank in question. The corresponding term is **NOSTRO** (Our Account), which refers to an account opened by the bank at another financial institution.

### Specific Definitions

**VOSTRO Account:**
Refers to an account that other banks open at this bank.

- *Example:* If Bank A (China) opens a USD account for Bank B (USA), then this account is considered a VOSTRO account for Bank A (it holds money belonging to Bank B).

**NOSTRO Account:**
Refers to an account that this bank opens at other banks.

- *Example:* If Bank A (China) opens a USD account at Bank B (USA), then this account is considered a NOSTRO account for Bank A (it holds money belonging to Bank A itself).

### Application Scenarios

**Cross-border Transfers:**

- When a bank processes cross-border remittances, it needs to transfer funds via its NOSTRO accounts (the bank's own accounts held overseas).
- The counterparty bank receives the funds via VOSTRO accounts (accounts held by the counterparty at this bank).

**Clearing and Reconciliation:**

- Banks manage settlement positions with other institutions through VOSTRO/NOSTRO accounts to ensure transparent fund flow.

**Foreign Exchange Trading:**

- Foreign exchange transactions often require currency conversion via NOSTRO accounts, while VOSTRO accounts may be used to record customer funds.

### Illustrative Example

**Case Study:**
Assume the **Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC)** opens a USD account at **Citibank (USA)** (for cross-border USD settlement). Then:

- For **ICBC**, this account is a **NOSTRO account** (Our Account).
- For **Citibank**, this account is a **VOSTRO account** (Your Account, holding ICBC's money).
